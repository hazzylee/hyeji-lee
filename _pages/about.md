I am Hyeji Lee, a postdoctoral researcher in the Department of Brain Convergence at the Korea Brain Research Institute (KBRI), Republic of Korea. I received my PhD in Psychology from the University of Edinburgh in 2026.

### Current Research
At KBRI, I study how cognitive control develops in children and how this development relates to everyday experience and to the brain. My current projects examine how smartphone use influences the development of cognitive control, and compare children with ADHD and typically developing children to understand how cognitive control development relates to brain structure and function.

I am also establishing a developmental EEG laboratory, including wireless EEG systems for laboratory research, portable EEG systems that can be used in educational settings, and an EEG hyperscanning setup for recording from multiple participants simultaneously.

### Doctoral Research
My doctoral research at the University of Edinburgh investigated cognitive control as a dynamic mechanism for learning across development, examining its role in different learning scenarios in children and adolescents using neuroimaging techniques (EEG and fNIRS). This research was supported by the Korean Government Scholarship Program for Study Overseas, Ministry of Education, South Korea, and a research support grant from the University of Edinburgh.

### Bridging Education & Development Science
Originally trained in Education, I maintain strong connections to educational policy while pursuing developmental science research. I have been working as a correspondent for the Korean Educational Development Institute since 2023, writing articles on UK educational policies for Korean scholars and policymakers. My research aims to discover how scientific insights on cognitive development can enhance educational interventions and inform policy decisions for supporting diverse learning needs.
